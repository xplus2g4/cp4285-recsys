# Recommendations as Treatments: Debiasing Learning and Evaluation

- **Authors:** Tobias Schnabel, Adith Swaminathan, Ashudeep Singh, Navin Chandak, Thorsten Joachims (Cornell)
- **Venue / year:** ICML 2016 (PMLR/JMLR W&CP vol. 48)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05352
- **Local PDF:** `docs/literature/papers/schnabel2016treatments.pdf`
- **Source basis:** full text (arXiv v2)

## Problem
Recommender training and evaluation data are Missing Not At Random (MNAR): users self-select what to rate, and the system decides what to show. The naive estimator (average loss over observed entries) is biased for MAE/MSE and for ranking metrics such as DCG@k and Prec@k. In a toy example it even ranks a clearly worse predictor above a better one.

## Method
- Treats a recommendation as a causal *treatment*. The observation matrix O is generated by an assignment mechanism with marginal propensities P_{u,i} = P(O_{u,i}=1) > 0 for all pairs.
- **IPS estimator** (Eq. 10): (1/UI) Σ_{O=1} δ_{u,i}/P_{u,i}. It is unbiased for any probabilistic assignment mechanism and needs only the marginals.
- **SNIPS estimator** (Eq. 11): divides by Σ_{O=1} 1/P_{u,i} instead of UI. It uses the control variate E[Σ 1/P] = UI, has lower variance and a small bias.
- **Propensity-scored ERM / MF-IPS** (Eq. 14): matrix factorisation in which each squared or absolute error is weighted by 1/P_{u,i}, trained with L-BFGS.
- Theory:
  - IPS tail bound (Prop. 3.1): the deviation grows as propensities become non-uniform. "We are paying for the unbiased-ness of IPS in terms of variability."
  - The generalisation bound (Thm 4.2) has a √(Σ 1/P²) term.
  - Lemma 5.1 gives the bias under wrong propensities: (1/UI) Σ δ·(1 − P/P̂).
  - Thm 5.2 shows a bias–variance trade-off: "it may be beneficial to overestimate small propensities, if this reduces the variability more than it increases the bias". This is the theoretical justification for clipping.
- **Propensity estimation:**
  - **Naive Bayes:** P(O=1|Y=r) = P(Y=r|O=1)P(O=1)/P(Y=r). It needs a small MCAR sample to estimate P(Y=r).
  - **Logistic regression** on user and item covariates. It needs no MCAR data.

## Assumptions held fixed
- Propensities are strictly positive, so every pair has full support. Observations are independent Bernoulli draws given P, which is needed for the variance bounds.
- O is independent of the new predictions Ŷ once observables are accounted for. Only the *selection* (exposure/observation) mechanism is corrected.
- The rating/label definition itself is held fixed. In the NB model, propensity depends only on the rating value r and is tied across all users and items, so item popularity and other item attributes are ignored. The LR model assumes the covariates make O independent of hidden factors and Y.
- The MCAR test set is treated as ground truth for evaluation. The rating model is otherwise unchanged; only the loss weights change.

## Experimental setup
- **Semi-synthetic ML100K:**
  - The matrix is completed with MF and re-quantised to the Marlin & Zemel rating marginals.
  - Observation model: propensity k for ratings 4–5 and k·α^{4−r} otherwise, with 5% of entries observed. α=1 is MCAR. α=0.25 matches the ML100K marginals.
  - Five synthetic predictors: REC_ONES, REC_FOURS, ROTATE, SKEWED, COARSENED.
- **Real data:**
  - **Yahoo! R3:** 15,400 users; the MNAR training set has "over 300K" ratings. The test set covers 5,400 users × 10 random songs. NB propensities are fit on 5% of the test set, and results are reported on the remaining 95%.
  - **Coat:** newly released with this paper. 290 Turkers, 300 coats, 24 self-selected plus 16 random ratings per user. LR propensities use user features (gender, age, location, fashion-awareness) and item features (gender, type, colour, promoted).
- **Baselines:** MF-Naive, HL-MNAR and HL-MAR (Hernández-Lobato et al. 2014).
- **Hyperparameters:** λ ∈ {10⁻⁶,…,1} and d ∈ {5,10,20,40}, selected by 4-fold CV *on MNAR data using the IPS estimator*.

## Key results
- **Table 1** (ML100K, α=0.25): the IPS and SNIPS means match the true values. For REC_ONES, true MAE is 0.102, IPS 0.102 ± 0.007, SNIPS 0.102 ± 0.007, Naive 0.011. The naive estimator mis-ranks predictors. SNIPS reduced the standard deviation for MAE but not for DCG.
- **Figure 2:** IPS and SNIPS are orders of magnitude more accurate than Naive over most α. At α=1 (MCAR), SNIPS equals Naive while IPS pays a small variance penalty.
- **Figure 3:** MF-IPS beats MF-Naive in MSE across α, and MF-IPS-NB still wins with badly estimated propensities. IPS-NB with estimated propensities sometimes beats known propensities, a stratification-like effect.
- **Table 2:**

  | Method | Yahoo MAE | Yahoo MSE | Coat MAE | Coat MSE |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | MF-IPS | 0.810 | 0.989 | 0.860 | 1.093 |
  | MF-Naive | 1.154 | 1.891 | 0.920 | 1.202 |
  | HL-MNAR | 1.177 | 2.175 | 0.884 | 1.214 |

  All differences are significant at p < 0.001.

## Reported limitations and trade-offs
- IPS variance grows with non-uniform propensities; small P gives large weights. The paper names propensity clipping (Strehl et al. 2010), doubly robust estimation and better propensity models as future improvements; it does not evaluate them.
- NB propensities need an MCAR sample, and NB ignores user and item dependence.
- The experiments use explicit ratings and rating-prediction losses. Ranking (DCG) is only evaluated as an estimator, not as a learning objective.

## Relevance to this project
- **§5 Exposure row:**
  - This paper is the origin of "IPS" and of the NB versus covariate (LR) propensity contrast. These map directly onto the spec's "popularity propensity" and "feature-based propensity".
  - It is also the source of the variance argument behind the clipping-threshold sweep.
- **§6 Exposure metrics:** Prop. 3.1 and Thm 4.2 motivate logging max weight, Σ1/P² (or effective sample size, ESS) and the standard deviation across seeds. This is the variance half of H2.
- **§2 / §10:** The paper uses a held-out MCAR slice (5% of the Yahoo test set) to fit propensities and reports results on the rest. This is analogous to our calibration/test split of the KuaiRand random log.
- **§10 Generalisation:** Coat was introduced here (Yahoo! R3 comes from Marlin & Zemel 2009) and is the replication target named for the popularity and exposure rows.
- **§4 Labels:** IPS corrects only the observation mechanism while holding the label definition fixed. This is consistent with the spec's separation between label change and reweighting.
